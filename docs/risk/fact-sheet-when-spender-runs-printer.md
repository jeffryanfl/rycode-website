## Thesis (one sentence)
If fiscal pressure bends rate policy toward cheap money, the slow bleed is inflation and financial repression: old fixed debt is paid with post-inflation dollars while households lose purchasing power — and trust, reserves, and payment rails can shift later.

## 2nd-, 3rd-, 4th-order effects (required — short)

**2nd-order (what happens next):** Bond buyers demand higher term premium or shorten duration; rolling maturities reprice into higher cash interest; “grow out of debt” via cheap money stalls if credit demand is weak.

**3rd-order (who else gets hurt):** Households and depositors lose real purchasing power (the mirror of the fiscal mechanism); credit unions / banks face NIMs, funding cost, and member stress; foreign official and private holders reassess duration and dollar exposure (watch TIC/COFER — do not invent a flight).

**4th-order (what systems shift later):** If rate policy is seen as fiscal relief first, repression becomes a habit; payment-stablecoin / bank digital-dollar rails concentrate who can move money under GENIUS-style rules; reserve managers keep diversifying at the margin — a quieter regime shift than a overnight crash headline.
